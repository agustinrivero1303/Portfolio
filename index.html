Robot Trainer (Final Year Master's Project)

2025 – 2026 · Dublin City University

A UR3e teaching rig that makes robot kinematics visible to students.
Problem / goal
Robotics theory is hard to connect to real hardware. The goal is a demonstrator students can use to validate kinematics theory from the DCU Robotics module.
My role and actions
I locked four of the UR3e's six joints so only the base and elbow move, derived its forward kinematics with the Denavit-Hartenberg convention, and reduced it to two symbolic transformation matrices. I programmed a four-waypoint pick-and-place in PolyScope and designed the student lab exercise around it.
Tech stack and methods
UR3ePolyScope 5URSimDenavit-HartenbergForward kinematicsHomogeneous transforms
Result
The robot performs four movements in a pick-and-place sequence. Each is a unique combination of the base angle θ1 and the elbow angle θ3, the only two moving joints. The other four joints stay fixed, so the six-axis UR3e behaves like a two-axis arm. For each waypoint, the kinematic model's predicted tool position was compared with the PolyScope teach pendant. The x and y positions matched within 0.3 to 14.3 mm. The z position was off by a consistent 25.8 to 29.9 mm, most likely because the model uses nominal rather than factory-calibrated DH parameters.
